# PULSE — Brand Guidelines

**Version:** 1.0  
**Category:** Personal finance / fintech  
**Positioning:** Money management built for real life, not finance insiders  
**Tagline:** *Make money make sense.*

> This is a fictional brand created as a realistic sample for design and marketing workflows.

## 1. Brand foundation

### Mission
Give people practical tools and clear explanations to better understand their day-to-day finances.

### Vision
A world where financial confidence is built through understandable information and useful habits.

### Values
- **Transparency:** Explain fees, limitations, and product conditions plainly.
- **Control:** Keep people in charge of their own decisions.
- **Simplicity:** Remove jargon without removing important detail.
- **Security:** Treat trust and data privacy as foundational.
- **Progress:** Celebrate realistic improvements, not perfection.

### Audience
- **Primary:** People aged 18–35 managing budgets, recurring costs, and savings goals.
- **Secondary:** Freelancers and early-career professionals balancing variable expenses.
- **Mindset:** Mobile-first, busy, financially curious, wary of hidden fees.

### Brand personality
Bold, straightforward, upbeat, trustworthy, useful.

## 2. Visual identity

### Logo
- Primary logo: lowercase `pulse` wordmark, geometric and slightly rounded.
- Secondary mark: abstract three-bar pulse motif.
- Logo may appear in Midnight or White only; a Lime icon can accompany but not replace the wordmark.
- Maintain clear space at least equal to the height of the letter `p`.
- Minimum digital width: 100 px for wordmark.
- Do not use the pulse motif as an implied medical endorsement.

### Color palette
| Role | Name | HEX | Usage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Midnight | `#101B32` | Backgrounds, headings |
| Primary | Electric Lime | `#CBF36A` | Brand accents, key actions |
| Secondary | Cloud | `#F4F7FB` | Light backgrounds |
| Secondary | Ocean | `#3567D4` | Illustrations and links |
| Neutral | Slate | `#536078` | Secondary text |

**Color rules:** Strong contrast and clear functional color hierarchy. Use Electric Lime primarily for a single dominant action per surface. Test all text combinations for accessibility; Lime is not suitable for every text size or background.

### Typography
- **Headlines:** Space Grotesk, Bold (fallback: Arial).
- **Body/UI:** Inter, Regular/Medium (fallback: Arial).
- Use large, expressive headlines with short supporting copy.
- Use tabular numerals where numerical comparisons need alignment.
- Avoid decorative fonts for monetary amounts or legal disclosures.

### Photography and illustration
- Real, relatable people and real-life money moments.
- UI-first graphics: simple dashboards, budget categories, annotated examples.
- Abstract geometry and clean motion-inspired lines are welcome.
- Avoid luxury-car imagery, cash piles, trading-floor clichés, or unrealistic wealth signals.

### Components
- Rounded cards and bold metric highlights.
- Simple outlined icons with consistent line weights.
- Graphs must include labels, units, and meaningful time frames.
- Never show invented account balances as actual user results; label mockups as illustrative.

## 3. Voice and messaging

### Tone of voice
Confident and encouraging without hype. Friendly, never flippant about money worries.

**We sound like:** "Know where your money goes."  
**We do not sound like:** "Get rich fast with one weird trick."

### Messaging pillars
1. **See clearly:** Understand spending and recurring costs.
2. **Stay in control:** Make decisions with useful context.
3. **Build momentum:** Small actions add up over time.

### Copy rules
- Lead with a useful action or outcome, then describe how it works.
- Define financial terminology at first use.
- Use specific language around features; avoid vague promises of savings.
- No guaranteed returns, debt elimination, or financial independence promises.
- Do not imply banking, deposit insurance, investment authorization, or security certifications unless verified.
- Disclose material conditions, fees, and eligibility where relevant.

### Sample copy
**Hero headline:** Your money. A clearer picture.  
**Supporting text:** See your spending patterns and plan your next move, all in one place.  
**CTA:** Explore how it works

**Social caption:** Three subscriptions you forgot about? A simple monthly review can help you spot recurring costs before they surprise you.

## 4. Channel guidelines

| Channel | Approach | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Website | Product-led messaging, straightforward FAQs | Hidden fee information |
| Mobile | Clear labels, accessible numbers, visible confirmations | Confusing money-movement flows |
| TikTok/Reels | Short educational explainers with examples | "Get rich quick" narratives |
| Email | Useful money tips and transparent updates | Artificial fear or urgency |
| Paid ads | One factual benefit per ad | Implied guaranteed savings |

## 5. Trust, privacy, accessibility
- Do not expose account details, transaction data, or personal information in creatives.
- Use fictitious or anonymized examples marked "Illustrative example."
- Financial performance examples must state assumptions and relevant caveats.
- Require human review for compliance-sensitive copy.
- Ensure color contrast, keyboard usability, readable charts, and accessible form errors.

## 6. Approval checklist
- [ ] Core message is understandable without financial expertise.
- [ ] Any numerical claim is sourced and contextualized.
- [ ] No financial guarantees or misleading investment claims.
- [ ] Pricing and key conditions are not obscured.
- [ ] Screenshots use mock or permissioned data.
- [ ] Primary CTA is obvious and accessible.
- [ ] Compliance review completed where required.

## 7. Non-negotiables
**Never:** promise guaranteed financial outcomes, fabricate security certifications, disguise paid content as impartial advice, use dark patterns, or depict private financial data without permission.

**Creative flexibility:** Be energetic with visual systems and phrasing, never careless with facts or trust.
